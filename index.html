A Second Passport

Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

Citizenship by investment gives you and your family a second passport in months rather than years, with wide visa-free travel. One thing to know before you compare prices: on CBI the cheapest route is usually the donation, and the donation is the one route that does not come back.

Read before you compare Caribbean programs

On 25 June 2026 the European Commission asked Antigua, Dominica, Grenada, Saint Kitts and Saint Lucia to end their citizenship programs by 1 June 2028, and running such a program is now in itself a ground for suspending Schengen visa-free travel. The governments have until 12 November 2026 to respond; Antigua has said it is leaning towards suspending its program by then. Separately, the United States has paused immigrant visas for nationals of all five countries since January 2026, and restricts visitor visas for Antigua and Dominica. None of this is final, but it changes the advice: a Caribbean passport bought today should be valued on what it is likely to offer after 2028, and we say so on every call. Vanuatu is not covered by the EU letter; its EU visa-free access was withdrawn earlier.

Read This First

Most of this money is not a fee. It is an investment you get back.

The most common misunderstanding in this industry is that the headline figure disappears. On most of these programs it does not. It buys you a fund unit, a title deed or a bond, and it comes back to you at the end of the holding period. Every card below says which part of your money comes back, which part is at risk, and which part is actually spent.

● Recoverable

Your capital stays yours. You hold a fund unit, a bond or a title deed, and you can exit it after the minimum holding period. Market value at exit is not guaranteed.

◔ At Business Risk

Deployed into an operating company or early-stage equity. Recoverable only through a sale or exit, and the business has to actually perform. Treat it as a real investment decision.

○ Contribution

A payment to the state or a national fund. It is not returned; it is what buys the status itself. Government, legal and due-diligence fees are never recoverable on any program.

Why People Do This

The Case for a Plan B

If everything you own, and everyone you love, depends on one passport and one government, you are exposed to decisions you do not control. A second residence or citizenship is insurance against that.

Most of our clients are not planning to leave. They want the option: a country the family can move to on short notice, a second banking and tax base, and travel that does not depend on visa appointments. Done properly, it also gives you a legal choice of where you are tax resident.

What It Gives You

  • Freedom to Move The legal right to enter and live in a second country whenever you choose, and in most cases wider visa-free travel for the whole family.
  • A Second Financial Base Bank accounts, property and a possible tax residence in a stable jurisdiction, so that one government's decisions never affect all of your assets at once.
  • Something Your Children Keep Citizenship passes to your children. Several residence programs let them study, work and eventually settle in their own right.

Knowledge Base

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I actually get my investment back?

On most programs, yes. It is the single most misunderstood point in this industry.

  • Recoverable routes. Fund units (Portugal, Hungary, Bulgaria), property (Greece, Cyprus, the Bahamas, Uruguay), government bonds (Saint Lucia, Italy) and a bank deposit (Malaysia) all leave you holding a real asset. After the minimum holding period you can redeem or sell it. Saint Lucia’s $300,000 bond returns 100% of your principal after five years, less a $50,000 government fee on that route that is not returned.
  • At-risk routes. Company equity (Latvia, Paraguay’s SUACE route, Italy’s startup route) is recoverable only if the business performs and you find a buyer.
  • Contributions. Caribbean donations, Malta’s government contribution and Portugal’s cultural-heritage route are payments, not investments. They are not returned.

Government, legal and due-diligence fees are never recoverable on any program, and market value at exit is never guaranteed on any investment route. Each program card above states its own position explicitly.

What is the difference between CBI and RBI?

Both are legal, government-run routes to a second status. They give you different rights, on different timelines, at different price points.

Citizenship by Investment (CBI): a second passport directly

  • What it is: You make a qualifying contribution or investment and receive full nationality and a passport. The usual routes are a non-refundable state contribution, an approved real estate purchase or a government bond.
  • The benefits: You have the same rights as any other citizen: to live and work there without limit, to vote (in most jurisdictions; not in Vanuatu), and to pass the citizenship on to your children.
  • Timeline and cost: Citizenship is usually granted within 2 to 18 months, Saint Lucia currently longer, with no language requirement and, as of today, little or no residence requirement. Minimums start at roughly USD 138,000 all-in. The cheapest route is almost always the donation, and that is the one route that is not returned.

Residency by Investment (RBI): the golden visa

  • What it is: A residence permit, often called a golden visa, that lets you and your family live, work and study in the country.
  • The benefits: It suits people who may actually want to live there, who want a second tax residence, or who want access to a region such as the Schengen Area. It does not give you a passport or a vote, at least not at first.
  • Timeline and cost: Entry points start at roughly €60,000 all-in, and the capital is far more often recoverable, because it sits in property or regulated funds rather than in a donation. Most residence programs lead to citizenship after 8 to 10+ years, usually with physical presence or language conditions attached. Some, such as Malta's MPRP and Malaysia's MM2H, are long-term residence only and not a route to citizenship.

In short: if you want a passport now and do not plan to move, CBI. If you want somewhere to live, a tax residence, or a gradual route to citizenship, RBI.

Are these programs legal and officially recognized?
Yes. Every program on this site is written into the law of the country that runs it, and applications go through the government unit responsible for it. We do not work with discretionary or unofficial routes. What does change is the terms: governments adjust prices, close routes and add requirements, which is why we check every program against the current rules before quoting it. The change to watch now: in June 2026 the EU asked the five Caribbean states to end their citizenship programs by 2028, and Antigua may suspend its program in November 2026. The programs remain legal; what is at stake is their future and their visa-free access to Europe.
Can I include my family members?
Yes. One application normally covers your spouse and dependent children, and on many programs your parents or parents-in-law as well. Age limits for children and the rules for adult children and parents differ by country, so tell us who you want included and we will check each program against your family.
Do I need to relocate to obtain the visa/passport?
Usually not. Most Caribbean citizenship programs have no residence requirement today: Antigua asks for 5 days in five years, Grenada's 30-day rule has been deferred, and the others have announced or proposed a 30-day rule under the new regional regulator. Among residence programs, Hungary has no minimum stay for its 10 year permit and Portugal asks for about seven days a year. The exception is a program built around actually living there: Malaysia's MM2H expects 90 days a year, which any family member can cover, while its premium visa (PVIP) has no minimum at all.
